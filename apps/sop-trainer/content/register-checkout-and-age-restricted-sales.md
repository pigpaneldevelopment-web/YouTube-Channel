# SOP: Front Register — Standard Checkout & Age-Restricted Sales

**Applies to:** Cashiers, shift leads, and any employee operating the front register at a convenience store or gas station.
**Products covered by age restriction:** Tobacco and vapor products, alcohol, lottery tickets.

## 1. Purpose
Ring up customer purchases accurately and, for any age-restricted product, confirm the customer is legally old enough to buy it before the sale completes. Getting this wrong isn't just a bad customer interaction — it's a compliance violation that can cost the store its license to sell tobacco, alcohol, or lottery products, and can carry personal liability for the employee in many states.

## 2. Definitions
- **Age-restricted product**: any item requiring ID verification before sale (tobacco/vapor, alcohol, lottery — verify your store's exact list, it varies slightly by state).
- **Acceptable ID**: unexpired, government-issued photo ID — driver's license, state ID card, passport, military ID. No exceptions for other forms (student ID, work badge, etc.).
- **Verification age**: the store's policy age for requiring an ID check — commonly "anyone who appears under 40," regardless of the legal purchase age, because visual age estimation is unreliable. Confirm your store's exact threshold; this SOP uses 40 as the standard default.

## 3. Standard Checkout Procedure
1. Greet the customer as they approach the register.
2. Scan each item. Confirm the price/description on screen matches the item as you scan — catch mis-scans before totaling, not after.
3. For any age-restricted item, **do not scan it yet** — go to Section 4 first, then return here once ID is verified.
4. State the total clearly before taking payment.
5. Process payment (cash, card, mobile pay per your register's supported methods).
6. For cash: count change back to the customer out loud.
7. Offer a receipt. Bag items if requested.
8. Thank the customer.

## 4. Age-Restricted Sale Procedure
1. When an age-restricted item is scanned, most registers will prompt for a birthdate entry or an ID check — **do not override or skip this prompt.**
2. If the customer visually appears under the store's verification age (default: under ~40), **ask for ID before scanning the item**, every time, no exceptions for regulars or people who "definitely look old enough."
3. Check the ID:
   - Confirm it's an acceptable ID type (Section 2).
   - Confirm it's not expired.
   - Calculate the customer's current age from the birthdate — don't rely on the printed age or eyeball it.
   - Confirm the photo reasonably matches the customer.
4. **If the customer meets the legal age and the ID checks out:** enter the birthdate into the register prompt (or confirm per your system), complete the scan, continue checkout normally.
5. **If the customer is underage, the ID is expired, unacceptable, or you have any doubt it's genuine:** refuse the sale. Use a neutral, non-accusatory script:
   > "I'm not able to sell this without valid ID that meets our age requirement — I can ring up everything else, or you're welcome to come back with ID."
6. Remove the age-restricted item from the transaction and continue checkout with the remaining items if the customer wants to proceed.

## 5. Exceptions & Edge Cases
- **Customer has no ID on them:** refuse the age-restricted item. No manager override exists for this — it's a hard stop.
- **ID looks altered, damaged, or fake:** refuse the sale. Do not confront the customer or accuse them directly; use the same neutral refusal script. If you're genuinely concerned it's fraudulent, notify a manager after the customer leaves — don't escalate in front of them.
- **Customer becomes argumentative or upset about being carded:** stay neutral, repeat the script once, and if it continues, call a manager over rather than engaging further yourself.
- **Register system is down / can't process the birthdate prompt:** do not manually override the age check by estimation. Hold age-restricted sales until the system is back, or escalate to a manager for the store's documented manual fallback procedure.
- **You personally know the customer and know their age:** still follow the ID check procedure. Personal knowledge doesn't replace the documented process — this protects you as much as it protects the store.

## 6. Escalation — When to Call a Manager
- Suspected fake or altered ID.
- Customer dispute or aggressive reaction to a refused sale.
- Register/system malfunction during an age-restricted transaction.
- Any till discrepancy discovered during or after a shift.
- Any situation not clearly covered by this SOP.

## 7. Compliance Note
Age-verification steps in this SOP are not optional and have no manager override for "no ID present." Violating age-restricted sale requirements can result in fines to the store, suspension or loss of the store's tobacco/alcohol/lottery license, and potential personal liability for the employee, depending on state law. When in doubt, refuse the sale and ask a manager — a lost sale costs far less than a compliance violation.
